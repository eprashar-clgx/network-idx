Validating an index that has no “right answer”
No dataset gives the true fiber opportunity for every parcel. Instead of one accuracy score, we test whether the model is stable, internally sensible, and directionally consistent with independent real-world data.

Lens 1 · Stability
Clustering held up

The grouping of areas into market types — the step that sets feature weights — stayed stable as we varied the number of clusters. Each cluster’s feature averages (z-scores) told a sensible, real-world story rather than arbitrary splits.

Stable & interpretable
Lens 2 · Balance
No feature dominates

Initial experiments showed the growth score tracking the overall score very closely (correlation ≈ 0.95) — distance-to-nearest-hotspot was pulling more than its share. After rebalancing the weights, the three buckets now contribute evenly.

Rebalanced
Lens 3 · Right signals
Top drivers are fiber

The model’s top three drivers are all fiber-based (FCC + Rextag): the fiber opportunity gap, top-tier fiber availability, and distance to the nearest fiber line — exactly what a fiber-opportunity index should key on.
